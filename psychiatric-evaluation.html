Psychiatric Evaluation: The Foundation of Personalized Care

A psychiatric evaluation is a critical first step in understanding your mental health needs. At The Place for Recovery, we conduct comprehensive evaluations that help us plan the right next step for your care.

What a psychiatric evaluation includes

  • Clinical interviews about mental health history, current symptoms, and stressors
  • Standardized assessments that measure emotional and psychological well-being
  • Medical history review to identify contributing health factors
  • Laboratory tests when indicated, with freedom to use any lab of choice

What to expect

  • A full psychiatric intake that is never rushed
  • Diagnosis explained clearly in plain language
  • Treatment options reviewed together so your input matters
  • Follow-up built into every plan from day one

Time frame and importance

Typically, a psychiatric evaluation lasts between 50 to 60 minutes. This in-depth process allows our clinicians to gain a holistic view of your mental health and create a clear, effective roadmap to recovery.

What we diagnose and treat

We assess a wide range of conditions to support clarity and long-term mental wellness.

Major Depressive Disorder Generalized Anxiety Panic Disorder Bipolar Disorder Schizophrenia OCD PTSD ADHD Autism Spectrum Disorders Eating Disorders Sleep Disorders Personality Disorders

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)

ADHD is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that can impact daily functioning.

  • Difficulty sustaining attention or following through on tasks
  • Impulsivity and difficulty waiting for one's turn
  • Hyperactivity, including fidgeting or talking excessively
  • Poor organizational skills and forgetfulness

Autism Spectrum Disorders (ASD)

ASD includes developmental conditions that affect communication, social interaction, and repetitive behaviors.

  • Difficulty with social interactions and understanding social cues
  • Limited verbal or non-verbal communication
  • Repetitive behaviors or routines
  • Intense focus on specific interests and sensory sensitivities

Personality Disorders

Personality disorders involve enduring patterns of thinking and behavior that affect relationships, identity, and emotional regulation.

  • Difficulty maintaining relationships
  • Rigid or unhealthy thought patterns
  • Intense emotional responses
  • Impulsivity or self-destructive behaviors
